# [C1] T-C1-84 · Parent Major Incident reference — Incident side only

## Context

`US-C1-20` requires an Incident being attached to a parent Major Incident to **store the parent reference**, and states the boundary without ambiguity: **declaring, coordinating and closing the Major Incident belongs to `C13` and is out of scope here**. The `user-stories.md` scope boundary repeats it: no story in this epic traces to an `FR-MIM-*`, and **no `FR-MIM-*` ticket is written in this epic**.

`ARCHITECTURE.md` §4.1 places `C1` and `C13` in the same `incident` context, so this is a same-context reference rather than a cross-context one — which is exactly why the temptation to implement the parent side here is worth naming and refusing.

## Scope

- Parent Major Incident reference on the `Incident` aggregate, using the typed link of `T-C1-81` with its own link kind.
- Invariants: an Incident has at most one parent Major Incident; a Major Incident cannot be its own parent.
- `IncidentAttachedToMajorIncident` event published post-commit.
- Out of scope, explicitly: Major Incident declaration (`FR-MIM-01`), the Major Incident protocol and accelerated SLA targets (`FR-MIM-02`), resolution and closure propagation (`FR-MIM-03`), the communication cadence (`FR-MIM-04`), the post-review record (`FR-MIM-05`) and the portal status message (`FR-MIM-06`). All belong to `C13`.

## Acceptance criteria

**Given** an Incident and an existing Major Incident **When** an agent attaches the Incident **Then** the parent reference is stored and one event is published after commit.

**Given** an Incident already attached to a parent **When** a second parent is attached **Then** it is refused with a typed domain error.

**Given** the attached Incident **When** the Major Incident is resolved or closed **Then** **nothing** happens to the child in this epic — propagation is `FR-MIM-03` and belongs to `C13`.

**Given** the `incident` libraries **When** they are searched **Then** they contain no Major Incident declaration, protocol, cadence or closure-gate behavior.
