# [C1] T-C1-61 · Requester-only authorization and the expired-window refusal

## Context

`US-C1-18` requires a user who is **not the requester** of the Incident to be denied by the visibility and ownership predicate (`FR-IAM-03`), **with the response not revealing whether the Incident exists**; and requires a confirm or reject **after** the window to be refused with a message stating that the Incident is closed and what to do instead (`NFR-USE-05`) — never a silent failure.

The visibility predicate and the not-found-indistinguishable outcome are `C10` (`T-C10-41`, `T-C10-42`); this ticket consumes them for the Incident aggregate. `C10` finding **F15** noted that end-to-end proof over real Incidents lands here — this is that proof.

## Scope

- Ownership check on both confirm and reject use cases through the `C10` requester-scoped visibility predicate.
- Denial rendered indistinguishably from a non-existent Incident: same status, same body, same timing characteristics as far as the response is concerned (`NFR-SEC-03`).
- Expired-window refusal as a distinct, explicit outcome naming the closed state and the next step, localized through `nestjs-i18n`.
- Out of scope: the predicate itself (`C10`); the auto-close (`T-C1-63`); the UI (`T-C1-62`).

## Acceptance criteria

**Given** a user who is not the requester and holds no competition-scoped grant **When** they confirm or reject **Then** the response is indistinguishable from the response for an Incident that does not exist.

**Given** the requester **When** they confirm or reject after the window has elapsed **Then** it is refused with a message stating that the Incident is closed and what to do instead, and the state is unchanged.

**Given** an agent rather than the requester **When** they attempt to confirm on the requester behalf **Then** it is denied — confirmation is the requester decision.

**Given** these denials **When** they occur **Then** nothing is persisted and no domain event is published.
