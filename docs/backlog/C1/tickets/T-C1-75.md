# [C1] T-C1-75 · Functional escalation as a distinct kind, with its permission gate

## Context

`US-C1-25` requires a functional escalation to route the Incident to the **target tier Resolver Group**, be **recorded as its own event distinct from an ordinary reassignment**, capture the assignment move in the history of `US-C1-24`, record actor, reason and timestamp, notify the receiving group through `C16`, and be denied with `403` for an actor without the escalation permission.

Distinctness is the load-bearing requirement: if escalation is just a reassignment, reporting cannot tell a routing correction from a tier escalation.

## Scope

- `EscalationKind` in `libs/incident/domain` with the functional kind, extended by `T-C1-77` with the hierarchical kind.
- `EscalateFunctionallyUseCase` in `libs/incident/application`, permission-gated, appending to the assignment history and marking the entry as an escalation.
- `IncidentEscalated` domain event carrying the kind, the target, the actor, the reason and the timestamp, published post-commit for `C16` and `C18`.
- Contract DTOs and the HTTP route.
- Out of scope: tier definition and group membership (`C14`); notification delivery (`C16`); the hierarchical kind (`T-C1-77`); the automatic trigger (`T-C1-78`).

## Acceptance criteria

**Given** an agent with the escalation permission **When** they escalate functionally with a reason **Then** the Incident is routed to the target tier group, the history entry is marked as an escalation, and one `IncidentEscalated` event carries kind, target, actor, reason and timestamp.

**Given** the resulting history **When** it is read **Then** the escalation is distinguishable from an ordinary reassignment without inspecting the reason text.

**Given** an actor without the escalation permission **When** they invoke it directly and through the route **Then** both are denied with `403` and nothing changes.

**Given** an escalation and a reassignment to the same group **When** both are recorded **Then** the two produce different event kinds, so reporting can separate them.
